Start with the drain, not the fixture

Supply lines are easy to move. Drains are not. A drain depends on gravity, so a toilet, sink or shower must sit above a line that slopes toward the main stack or sewer. In a basement, that line may be below the level of the sewer, which changes the plan.

A plumber can check where the main drain runs and whether a bathroom can drain by gravity or needs a sewage ejector pump. That one question decides cost, layout and noise. Ask it before you fall in love with a floor plan. New work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted under Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code, and those standards set drain slope, venting and fixture clearances.

Installation follows the construction schedule.

    The plumber walks the space and checks drain paths, venting and supply.

Permits and inspections

New plumbing work generally needs a permit. Bensalem Township has opted into Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code, and its Building and Planning office handles plan review, permits and inspections. Call 215-633-3644 to ask what your project needs.

Rough-in inspection happens before walls close. Missing it can mean opening finished walls later, so build it into the schedule. A contractor who is clear about the permit steps is a good sign.

Working with other trades

Plumbing sits between framing, electrical and finish work. A good plan puts the rough-in after framing and before insulation and drywall. Pulling the plumber in early avoids a joist cut that no one planned for or a wall that is too thin for a drain.

If you are hiring a general contractor, ask who is responsible for permits and inspections. For home improvements in Pennsylvania, the Attorney General's registration line at 1-888-520-6680 lets you check a contractor's registration.
