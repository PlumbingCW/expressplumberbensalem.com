What Cornwells Heights is

The Census Bureau recognizes Cornwells Heights as a census-designated place of about 0.47 square miles, with 1,251 residents in 2020. Until 2010 it was combined with its neighbor Eddington in a single area that the 2000 census counted at 3,406 people and 1,239 housing units. That earlier description called it mostly middle-class single-family homes on grid-style streets.

A local guide describes Cornwells Heights as anchored by the train station, with residential streets and an older, tight-knit feel. The station serves SEPTA's Trenton Line and Amtrak's Keystone Service. Because exact boundaries differ between maps, this page uses the station and I-95 as anchors instead of drawing a border.

What to do when a leak starts and you are away from home

Commuters often learn about a leak by phone or on arriving home. Having a plan makes the first ten minutes calmer.

  1. 1

    Shut off the water

    Close the main shutoff as soon as you can. A household member or neighbor who knows where it is can do this.

  2. 2

    Protect the floors

    Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the wet area.

  3. 3

    Switch off a leaking heater

    Close the cold supply valve on a leaking water heater and turn off its power or gas control.

  4. 4

    Take photos

    Photos of the leak and any damage help when you speak with a plumber or your insurer.

  5. 5

    Call a plumber

    Describe what you found and where the shutoff is. That lets the plumber plan tools and parts.

Housing information for the area

NeighborhoodScout's tract profile for Eddington and Cornwells Heights says many homes were built between 1940 and 1969, with some built from 1970 to 1999. It lists mostly small to medium single-family homes, plus apartment complexes, and says most of the housing is owner occupied. These are tract-level figures from one source, so they describe the area in general, not your house.

A house of any age can have fixtures replaced at different times. The best guide to your plumbing is a quick look at the shutoff valves, the water heater tag and the pipes visible in the basement or utility area.
