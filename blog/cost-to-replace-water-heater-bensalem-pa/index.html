Average Cost of Replacing a Water Heater: A Bensalem, PA Price Guide

By the Bensalem Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026. Updated November 14, 2026.

The cost of replacing a water heater depends on the type of unit, the fuel, the venting and how hard the install is. A simple like-for-like tank swap sits at the low end. A move to tankless or a change of fuel sits at the high end. A published average cannot tell you what your house will cost.

This guide breaks down what goes into the price, so you can read a quote and ask the right questions. It does not quote dollar figures, because the honest number comes from seeing your water heater.

The main cost drivers

Each line below moves the final price.

DriverLower costHigher cost
Type of heaterStandard tankTankless or heat pump
Fuel and conversionSame fuel as beforeSwitching from electric to gas or the reverse
VentingReuse a sound flueNew power vent or sealed vent
Tank sizeMatches the existing unitLarger tank or more capacity
AccessBasement with clear stairsAttic, crawl space or tight closet

Tank versus tankless

A standard tank is the lowest upfront choice, and the swap is straightforward when the old unit used the same fuel and vent. Tankless units cost more up front, and the install often needs a larger gas line and new venting. They also take less space and can last longer with service.

Heat pump water heaters use electricity to move heat and can lower running costs, but they need room and airflow and they cool the space they sit in. We explain the options on our installation page and our tankless page.

Items a good quote itemizes

Ask to see each of these in writing.

  • ✓The water heater model, capacity and warranty.
  • ✓New shutoff valve, connectors and a relief valve line.
  • ✓Expansion tank if the system needs one.
  • ✓Venting changes and gas work if any.
  • ✓Permit, inspection and disposal of the old unit.

Emergency versus planned replacement

A heater that fails at night leaves little room to compare. A planned swap lets you choose the unit, the time and the scope. If your heater is well past a decade old, it may be wise to plan the swap before it fails.

An after-hours replacement carries a different labor cost than a daytime job, because staffing costs are different. If a tank is leaking, shut the supply, protect the floor and call. The water heater replacement page covers what happens next.

Local note: permits, registration and who to call

Permit rules for water heater swaps vary. Bensalem Township's Building and Planning office at 215-633-3644 can tell you what applies at your address. In Pennsylvania, a contractor who does $5,000 or more of home improvement work in a year must register with the Attorney General, and you can verify at 1-888-520-6680.

These checks protect you. Ask for a written scope with your quote. If you live in Levittown or Feasterville-Trevose, ask the plumber which township office covers your street, since permit offices depend on the municipality.

How to compare two quotes

Line up the same items on each quote. Check the heater's capacity and warranty, then the extras. A lower total may leave out a permit, a venting fix or removal of the old unit. Ask each plumber what is not included.

Price is only one part of the decision. Ask how long the work takes, what happens if something unexpected turns up and who is responsible for the permit. A clear written scope is the best defense against surprise charges.

Ways to keep the final bill from surprising you

Ask the plumber to look at the venting, the gas line and the shutoff valve before quoting. These are the places where hidden work lives. A quote that includes them is more likely to match the final bill.

Ask what happens if the plumber finds a problem after the old heater is out. A good contractor explains the change and gets your approval before adding to the scope.

Questions to ask about the new heater

These take a minute and tell you a lot.

  • ✓What is the first-hour rating, not just the tank size?
  • ✓What does the warranty cover on the tank and on parts?
  • ✓Does the unit need a new drain pan or discharge line?
  • ✓Will the plumber label the shutoff valves and show you the temperature setting?
  • ✓Who do I call if something goes wrong in the first month?
