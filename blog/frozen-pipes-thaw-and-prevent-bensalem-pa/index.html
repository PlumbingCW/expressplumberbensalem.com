Frozen Pipes: How to Thaw Them and Prevent Bursts in Bensalem, PA

By the Bensalem Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026. Updated November 14, 2026.

If a faucet runs slowly or not at all in freezing weather, a pipe may be frozen. Open the faucet, find the cold section and warm it gently with a hair dryer or warm towels. Never use an open flame. If you find a split or water appears, shut the main valve and call a plumber.

This guide covers how to thaw a pipe safely, what mistakes to avoid and how to prevent the next freeze. It treats a burst pipe as an emergency, because it is.

How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

Work slowly, and keep heat moving.

  1. 1

    Open the faucet

    Open the tap served by the frozen pipe so water can flow as it thaws.

  2. 2

    Find the frozen section

    Look for a pipe that is cold, frosted or bulging, usually along an exterior wall or in an unheated space.

  3. 3

    Apply gentle heat

    Use a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels. Start near the faucet and work toward the frozen spot.

  4. 4

    Check for leaks

    Once water runs, look along the pipe for drips or wet spots.

Things you must not do

Never use a propane torch, a kerosene heater or any open flame on a pipe. It can start a fire inside the wall, and it can scorch the pipe. Do not leave a heat source unattended.

If you find a split, act quickly. Call the line at any hour, and see burst pipe repair for the steps. Pipe insulation should follow the maker's guidelines, and any repaired section is held to International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards.

  • !Close the main shutoff valve if water is leaking.
  • !Do not thaw a pipe near the electrical panel if the area is wet.
  • !Keep heat sources away from insulation and stored items.
  • !Do not ignore a pipe that stays frozen after you thaw it.

Where pipes freeze

Cold spots follow a pattern.

Prevention before the cold arrives

Most freezes are preventable.

  • ✓Disconnect garden hoses and shut interior valves to outdoor spigots.
  • ✓Insulate exposed pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls.
  • ✓Keep the thermostat set to a steady temperature, even if you travel.
  • ✓Let a faucet drip during an extreme cold snap.
  • ✓Learn where your main shutoff valve is before you need it.

Local note: heat failure and the water meter

Pipes freeze fastest when the heat is off. If your boiler or furnace fails during a cold snap, see boiler repair and call at once. Your water utility can tell you where your meter shutoff sits and who handles it, so look at your water bill for the utility's name.

Homeowners in Langhorne and Feasterville-Trevose with older homes should check exterior walls, since those are the common freeze points. We do not state a local frost depth because it varies by site. Ask the township's Building and Planning office at 215-633-3644 if you are planning buried line work.

What happens after the thaw

A pipe that froze once is at risk of freezing again, and it may have a hairline crack that only leaks when the water is on. After you thaw it, run the tap and watch the pipe for several hours. Check the ceiling and walls below for stains.

If you find a leak later, shut the water and call. A plumber can repair or replace the damaged section. If freezes keep happening in the same spot, the answer is insulation or rerouting, and a plumber can advise.

Going away for the winter

An empty house in January is a risk. If the heat fails, no one is there to notice. Set the thermostat to a steady temperature, and ask someone to check the house if you travel for several days.

Some homeowners shut off the main valve and drain the lines. That is a good option for a long absence, and a plumber can show you how. Do not drain a system with a boiler or a sprinkler without proper steps.

Supplies worth having before the cold arrives

These are cheap and easy to store.

  • ✓Foam pipe insulation sleeves in the common sizes.
  • ✓A hair dryer or a heating pad reserved for pipes.
  • ✓A flashlight and a few towels near the shutoff valve.
  • ✓A phone number for a plumber on the refrigerator.
  • ✓A set of cabinet door magnets or stops to keep doors open.
