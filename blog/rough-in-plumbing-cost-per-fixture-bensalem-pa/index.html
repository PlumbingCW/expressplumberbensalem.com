Rough-In Plumbing Cost per Fixture: A Bensalem, PA Homeowner Guide

By the Bensalem Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026. Updated November 14, 2026.

Rough-in plumbing is the supply and drain piping installed inside walls and floors before the finishes go on. Cost per fixture depends on how far the lines run, whether the drain can use gravity and how hard the space is to reach. A flat number per fixture is a rough guide at best.

This guide explains what a rough-in includes and what moves the price, so you can compare quotes fairly. We do not quote dollar figures here, because the honest number comes from seeing the space.

What rough-in actually includes

A rough-in puts the pipes in place and stops short of the fixtures. It includes drain lines with the right slope, vent lines, hot and cold supply lines to the spot where each fixture will sit, shutoff valves and a pressure test. Fixtures, faucets and trim come later.

Inspection is part of the process. Most jurisdictions require the rough-in to be inspected before the walls are closed. A plumber who understands the local process builds it into the schedule so that drywall does not wait. Rough-in dimensions come from the International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards and from each fixture maker's guidelines.

What changes the price of a fixture

These factors explain why two fixtures can cost very different amounts.

Distance from the main stackFixture near existing linesFixture at the far end of the house

How to compare rough-in quotes

Ask each plumber the same questions so the numbers line up.

  • ✓Does the quote include the permit and the inspection?
  • ✓Which fixtures and how many drain, vent and supply lines are included?
  • ✓Does it include an ejector pump if the drain is below the sewer?
  • ✓Who patches walls, floors or concrete after the work?
  • ✓What happens if the plumber finds something unexpected behind the wall?

The basement bathroom question

A basement bath is the most common rough-in that changes the cost. If the sewer lateral runs above the basement floor, gravity will not carry waste out of the house, and a sewage ejector pump is needed. The pit, the pump and the sealed lid all add to the scope.

If the lateral is lower than the floor, a gravity drain may work, but cutting a trench in the slab is still real work. A plumber can check where your lateral exits and tell you which case you have. For the planning side, read about plumbing installation.

Local note: permits and registered contractors

Rough-in work in Bensalem generally needs a permit. The township's Building and Planning office at 2400 Byberry Road handles plan review, permits and inspections, and its inquiry line is 215-633-3644. Ask what applies before you accept a quote that leaves the permit out.

Pennsylvania's Attorney General also requires contractors who do $5,000 or more of home improvement work in a year to register, and you can verify at 1-888-520-6680. If you are remodeling in Croydon or Bristol, confirm which township office covers your address.

How to avoid surprises

Ask for a written scope that lists each fixture and what is included. Photograph the walls and floors before work starts. If the plumber finds old, damaged or undersized pipe, ask for a written change order before extra work begins.

Choose your fixtures before rough-in if you can. The rough-in dimensions depend on the model, and changing a toilet or a vanity later can mean moving pipes. If your project includes a new water heater, see water heater installation so capacity matches the new load.

Why plumbers hesitate to quote by the fixture

A per-fixture price sounds simple, but two toilets in the same house can differ widely. One sits above the main stack and the other sits at the end of a long run that needs a new vent. A fair quote looks at each location.

If a quote comes back as a single number with no breakdown, ask for the scope. A clear line item list protects you from misunderstandings about what is included.

Documents worth keeping after rough-in

Save these for later.

  • ✓Photos of the open walls with pipe routes visible.
  • ✓The permit and the inspection sign-off.
  • ✓A sketch of where shutoff valves and cleanouts sit.
  • ✓The fixture model numbers and installation sheets.
  • ✓The written scope and any change orders.
