When installation is a planning project

Planned installs come up in a few situations. A finished basement is being converted, an addition is going in, the old heater is sound but oversized, or the household wants to leave oil or electric for gas. Each one opens a different set of choices.

The big question is whether the new location and fuel can support the unit. A power-vent tank needs an electrical outlet and a way to exhaust sideways. A tankless needs a gas line sized for its burner and a vent run that clears the house.

Settle those before buying equipment. Installation follows the maker's guidelines and International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards for the relief valve, discharge pipe and venting, so ask to see them in the scope.

Venting and combustion air

Gas units must exhaust combustion gases safely, and the vent path is the most frequently mishandled part of an install. In many homes a water heater shares a flue with a furnace or boiler, and connecting a new unit to an old flue without checking its size is a known hazard.

Installers should check the flue size for the combined load or choose a direct vent. A carbon monoxide detector near the sleeping area is inexpensive protection on any home with fuel-burning equipment, and it is smart to confirm that detectors are working on install day.

Bensalem permits, codes and contractor checks

Bensalem Township enforces the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code and uses the International Plumbing Code. Call the township's Building and Planning office at 215-633-3644 to ask what a water heater install requires at your address, because permit rules vary.

Hire a contractor who is open about this. Pennsylvania requires contractors who perform $5,000 or more in home improvement work annually to register with the Attorney General, and you can verify a registration at 1-888-520-6680. It takes a minute and filters out a lot of risk.
