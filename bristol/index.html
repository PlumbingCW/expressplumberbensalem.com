Why age shapes plumbing in Bristol

Bristol was settled in 1681 and is the third-oldest borough in Pennsylvania, with a charter that is still in effect. Its blocks include several historic districts, among them the Bristol Historic District, Bristol Industrial, Harriman and Jefferson Land Association. A walk through them shows buildings from many eras side by side.

For a homeowner, that mix has a practical meaning. A house that has been renovated several times may have copper, galvanized steel, plastic and cast iron all in the same wall. Each material ages differently, and each fails in its own way.

When a plumber opens a wall, the first job is to find out what is actually there. Older houses are often brought up to International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards when plumbing is replaced, so ask the borough what applies.

Local note: the river, the canal and what water means here

Bristol sits on the Delaware River, opposite Burlington, New Jersey, and is the terminus of the Delaware Canal, a National Historic Landmark District. US 13, also called Bristol Pike, and PA 413 run through the borough. Waterfront and low-lying blocks near the river are worth a thought if you store things in a basement.

We do not state a flood history for individual streets, because that depends on the block. If your basement takes on water in heavy rain, the sump pump page explains the repair side, and your borough office can tell you about local drainage.

Planning ahead in an older house

If your Bristol house has not had its plumbing looked at in years, a calm visit beats an emergency one. A plumber can trace the shutoff valves, test pressure and look at the pipe materials. That visit gives you a map of what you own before something fails.

Write down what you learn. A short list of where the main shutoff sits and what the pipes are made of will help the next plumber, and it helps you in an emergency when you are rushed.
