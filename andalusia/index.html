Where Andalusia sits in Bensalem

Public references place Andalusia in the far south of the township. Woodhaven Road (PA Route 63) runs along its northeast edge, the Delaware River forms its eastern and southern edge, and Poquessing Creek marks the line toward Philadelphia on the north and west. Bristol Pike (US Route 13) is its main road.

The neighborhood takes its name from the Andalusia estate, the former home of financier Nicholas Biddle and a National Historic Landmark. The Pen Ryn mansion and the Katharine Drexel Shrine are also in the area. Boundaries on informal maps vary, so we describe the area by these anchor roads rather than by a fixed line.

A neighborhood with homes from many eras

Local guides describe Andalusia as a mix of grand riverfront houses and classic bungalows on older, quiet streets. One real estate guide notes that the earliest homes were 1800s estates, with small single-family houses on grid streets added in the 1920s. NeighborhoodScout's census-tract profile for the area says many homes were built between 1940 and 1969, with some built later, and that the housing includes single-family homes and apartment complexes occupied by both owners and renters.

What this means for you is simple. Age alone does not tell anyone what is inside your walls or under your yard. The only way to know your pipe materials, your main shutoff and your sewer line condition is to look at them.

Know your house before something goes wrong

A short walk-through now saves time later, especially in a house with a long history of repairs.

  • ✓Find the main water shutoff and make sure it turns.
  • ✓Note where the water heater sits and how old it is.
  • ✓Write down any past plumbing repairs and who did them.
  • ✓Check under sinks and around the water heater for slow drips.
  • ✓Ask a plumber to confirm the pipe materials the next time one is at the house.

A note on permits and inspections

For every service we offer across the township, see our Bensalem plumbing page. Bensalem Township's Building and Planning Department handles plumbing plan reviews, permits and inspections, and its office can be reached at 215-633-3644 to ask whether a job needs a permit. Rules differ by job, so ask before you begin larger work such as a water heater change, new fixtures or sewer repairs.
