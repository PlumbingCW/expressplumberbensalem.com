Why disposals jam, hum and quit

A humming disposal with no spin means the blades are stuck, often on a bone, a fruit pit or a utensil that fell in. The motor is trying to turn and cannot. Pressing the reset button and turning the shaft with a hex key from underneath frees many jams.

If the unit has no power at all, the cause may be a tripped reset, a switch or the circuit. A unit that has been struggling for years may simply be worn out. A plumber can test the motor and tell you which it is. A new disposal has to connect to the drain under IPC standards, including a proper trap, and the maker's guidelines cover the electrical side.

What to do when water backs up in the sink

A sink full of water that will not drain through the disposal is a clog in the unit or the drain beyond. Do not run the disposal. Running it against a blocked line can burn out the motor.

Call the line at any hour if water is rising or leaking into the cabinet.

  • !Turn off the disposal switch and unplug it, or switch off the breaker.
  • !Never put your hand in the disposal, even when off.
  • !Do not pour chemical cleaner into standing water.
  • !Place a bucket under any leak and empty the cabinet.

What not to put in a disposal

Most jams come from the same few items.

  • ✓Bones, fruit pits and corn cobs.
  • ✓Grease, oil and fat, which coat the pipe beyond the unit.
  • ✓Stringy vegetables such as celery and onion skins in large amounts.
  • ✓Coffee grounds and eggshells in quantity.
  • ✓Potato peels and rice, which turn to paste and plug the line.

Repair or replace

A jam clears quickly and costs little. A burned motor or a leaking housing means replacement. If the unit is old and has been repaired before, replacing it often costs about the same as another repair and gives you a new warranty.

When you replace, a plumber will check the drain trap and the dishwasher connection at the same time, since they share the unit's discharge. Clearing the line then is much easier with the disposal out of the way.
