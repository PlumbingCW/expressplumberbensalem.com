About the neighborhood

Real estate guides describe Neshaminy Valley as a quiet suburban neighborhood with townhomes, split-level homes and colonial-style houses. They list the Growden Mansion, a landmark with Revolutionary War ties, and Firefighter's Field Community Park among its local points of interest.

Sources disagree on when the homes were built. One local guide says most were built between 1969 and 1987, while another lists some 1920s homes and newer construction from the early 2000s. We therefore do not tie any plumbing advice to a construction year.

Townhome plumbing: what is different

In a townhome, a leak can reach a neighbor quickly. Know where your shutoff is, tell the association or property manager about a leak that may affect shared walls, and ask about the association's rules before you start repairs.

Water heaters in townhomes often sit in a closet or basement utility space, so tell the plumber how to reach it when you call.

Checking a split-level or basement before wet weather

These steps are general. They do not mean every home in the neighborhood has a sump pump or basement water problem.

  1. 1

    Look at the lowest level

    Check the floor and the walls for damp spots or stains.

  2. 2

    Test the sump pump if you have one

    Lift the float or pour in water to see that the pump starts.

  3. 3

    Clear the discharge

    Make sure water can leave the pipe and flow away from the house.

  4. 4

    Know the shutoff

    Be able to close the main water valve quickly.
