What to do when a business plumbing problem is an emergency

A leaking ceiling over inventory, a kitchen drain that will not clear before service or a bathroom that must close all qualify as emergencies. Call at any hour. Same-day service is offered when the schedule allows.

Staff can take a few steps while waiting, and they help protect the building and the customers. Commercial work is judged against the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code, which set standards for fixture counts, drains and backflow protection.

  • !Locate and close the main shutoff valve, and make sure staff know where it is.
  • !Move stock, paper records and electronics away from the wet area.
  • !Place signs at wet floors and close affected restrooms.
  • !Photograph damage and note the time for the insurance claim.

Why scheduled maintenance beats emergency calls

A restaurant that jets its grease lines on a schedule rarely faces a Friday night backup. An office that checks its shutoff valves and fixtures twice a year finds small leaks before they become flooded suites. The cost of maintenance is predictable, and the cost of an emergency is not.

We can discuss a maintenance plan after a first visit. A camera inspection of the building lateral, as described on the sewer inspection page, is a good starting point for an older property.

Working around business hours

Most businesses want repairs outside open hours, and some need immediate work during them. We discuss timing with you before scheduling, so customers and staff are disturbed as little as possible.

Tell us your opening hours, whether the space is shared and who can authorize work. In multi-tenant buildings, the property manager may need to approve access to shared lines, and knowing that up front prevents delays.

Permits and compliance

Commercial plumbing work often needs a permit and an inspection. Bensalem Township's Building and Planning office at 215-633-3644 can tell you what applies to your project, and a good contractor asks the question before starting.

Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act applies to home improvement work on residences, so confirm the rules for your specific property type with the township. Ask for a written scope and price, and keep it with your records.
