Why pumps fail in the worst weather

A sump pump sits idle most of the year and runs hardest during storms. That is when a stuck float switch, a clogged intake, a burned motor or a power outage shows up. A pump that worked last spring can fail this one.

Maintenance that prevents failures

Test the pump before the rainy season, not during it.

  • ✓Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump starts and stops.
  • ✓Clear debris from the pit and check the intake screen.
  • ✓Confirm the discharge pipe runs away from the foundation.
  • ✓Test the check valve so water does not flow back into the pit.
  • ✓Replace an old pump and battery on a regular schedule.

Discharge, drainage and what the pump cannot fix

A pump moves water out of the pit. It does not stop water from entering the basement through cracks or the floor joint. If water is seeping in even when the pump works, the problem may be drainage around the foundation, as discussed on the basement waterproofing page.

The discharge line matters too. If it empties near the foundation, the water cycles back into the pit. Extend it away from the house and check that it does not freeze or clog in winter.
