Cleaning versus repairing

Cleaning removes what is in the pipe. Repair fixes the pipe itself. If a camera shows a crack, a belly where water pools or a joint pulled apart, cleaning only buys time, and the stoppages come back.

That is why a repair conversation always begins with a recorded camera pass. You see the damage and so does the plumber. If the line is clean but sound, you leave with a cleaning.

If it is broken, you leave with options. Repair methods such as lining and open-cut replacement are held to International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards for materials, bedding and slope.

A failed sewer line at night

A main line that has failed can send wastewater into the house or into the yard. Both are urgent. Call at any hour, and a plumber can often be scheduled for the same day when the schedule allows.

Until help arrives, reduce the load on the line.

  • !Stop using water throughout the house.
  • !Keep people and pets out of any area with standing wastewater.
  • !Avoid chemical cleaners. They do not fix a broken pipe.
  • !Locate your cleanout if you can, and note any wet spots in the yard.

These symptoms point past a simple clog.

  • ✓Backups that return within weeks of a professional cleaning.
  • ✓A camera shows cracks, offsets or a pipe that has lost its round shape.
  • ✓A sunken strip or soggy patch in the yard above the line.
  • ✓Gurgling in drains when a distant fixture runs.
  • ✓Sewage odor outdoors near the cleanout or lawn.

Roots, age and the pipe in your yard

Roots seek moisture and find it at the tiny gaps in old joints. Once inside, they grow and widen the gap, so a clean line gets worse each year. Older clay and cast iron pipe joints are the most common entry points, while newer plastic pipe is tighter but can still shift.

If the camera shows root intrusion, the pipe may need to be cut and then lined or replaced. Clearing roots with a jet gives temporary relief, as covered on the hydro jetting page. Repair is what stops the cycle.

Who owns what, and what to ask the township

In general, the homeowner is responsible for the lateral from the house to the connection at the main in the street, though rules differ by municipality. Bensalem Township's Building and Planning office at 215-633-3644 can tell you where responsibility stops at your address and whether a permit is needed for the repair.

Ask before digging. Pennsylvania's one-call system requires utility marking before excavation, so a contractor should call it before breaking ground. Hire only contractors who are open about permits and registrations.
