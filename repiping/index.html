Repiping in Bensalem, PA

Repiping in Bensalem replaces the supply lines that bring water to your fixtures when the old ones leak, corrode or deliver rusty water. It is a planned project, not a patch, and it is worth considering when leaks keep appearing in different places.

If you have had several pinhole leaks, discolored water or pressure that has slowly dropped, call us to talk through the choices. We serve Bensalem, Cornwells Heights, Eddington, Andalusia and Trevose, and you get a clear price before work starts.

When repiping makes sense

A single leak is a repair. Three leaks in two years is a pattern. Pipes fail from the inside out as they age, so the next leak is rarely far from the last one, and each repair costs a visit and some drywall.

Other signs include brown or rusty water when a tap first opens, low pressure at several fixtures and visible green corrosion or white scale at joints. If these show up together, a plumber can inspect the pipe and tell you how much of the system is at risk. A repipe is done to International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards, and each material, whether PEX, copper or CPVC, also has its own installation guidelines from the maker.

Pipe materials compared

Each material has strengths and limits. The right one depends on the house.

MaterialStrengthsLimits
PEXFlexible, fewer joints, quick to installNot for outdoor use in sunlight, needs proper fittings
CopperLong record, rigid, heat tolerantHigher material cost, can corrode in aggressive water
CPVCLower cost, resists corrosionBrittle with age and heat

How a repiping project runs

Most projects follow four phases.

  1. 1

    Inspect and plan

    The plumber traces the existing lines and decides full or partial replacement.

  2. 2

    Scope and price

    You get a written scope covering materials, access holes and wall repair.

  3. 3

    Replace the lines

    New lines run through walls, floors or the attic, then connect to fixtures.

  4. 4

    Test and patch

    The system is pressure tested before the walls are closed up.

Living in the house during the work

Most repipes keep the house livable. Water is shut off during the working day and restored at night in many cases, and the crew cuts small access openings rather than opening whole walls. Plan for a few days of noise and dust.

Move belongings away from the walls where work will happen. If you have a slab foundation, mention it at the start, since the routing changes. A slab leak is often what pushes a homeowner into this decision, as discussed on the leak detection page.

Permits and contractor checks in Bensalem

Bensalem Township's Building and Planning office handles plan review, permits and inspections for plumbing work. Call 215-633-3644 to learn what applies at your address, and ask your contractor who pulls the permit.

In Pennsylvania, anyone offering home improvements who does $5,000 or more of work in a year must register with the Attorney General. Check a contractor at 1-888-520-6680 before signing. A written contract that lists scope, price and payment steps protects both sides.
