When a hidden leak cannot wait

Water you can hear but cannot see is already doing damage. A leak behind a ceiling can bring down drywall, and a leak under a slab can undermine flooring. If water is pooling, dripping through a ceiling or running at the meter, treat it as urgent.

The line is answered at any hour, and same-day service is offered when the schedule allows. These steps limit the damage while you wait. Repairs after a leak is found follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and any pipe that is opened up should be pressure tested to the same standards before it is closed.

  • !Shut off the main water valve and see whether the sound or the dripping stops.
  • !Place a bucket under active drips and move furniture out of the wet zone.
  • !Switch off power to any room where water is near outlets or light fixtures.
  • !Photograph the damage and the meter reading for your insurer.

How we find a leak without tearing things open

Detection works by narrowing the search step by step instead of guessing.

Slab leaks: what they are and what to do

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe that runs in or under a concrete floor slab. It is most often a hot or cold supply line, and it can cause warm floors, a sudden rise in the water bill or the sound of running water with fixtures off. Homes built on a slab have no basement to carry the water away, so the damage spreads under flooring.

A pressure test and a thermal or acoustic scan locate the line without breaking the slab first. The options then run from rerouting the line through the walls or ceiling to repairing it in place. For the cases where several lines are failing, read about repiping before agreeing to a string of small repairs.

Water meter and high-bill checks

A sudden jump on the water bill is often the first sign of a leak. The meter gives you a free test: turn off every tap and appliance, then watch the low-flow indicator on the meter. If it moves, water is leaving the system somewhere.

The usual suspects are a toilet that runs, an irrigation line or a leaking service line. A toilet dye test costs almost nothing. If the toilet is clean and the meter still moves, the leak is likely between the meter and the house, and a water service line repair may be next. Your water utility can also tell you whether your usage pattern changed, so ask for the account history.

Ceiling and wall leaks in Bensalem homes

Stains on a ceiling are rarely just where the leak starts. Water travels along joists and pipes before it shows, so the stain can sit several feet from the source. A moisture meter and a few controlled fixture tests find the true origin, which often turns out to be a bathroom supply line, a tub drain or a roof issue.

Do not paint over a stain until the source is confirmed. If the leak turns out to be a failing supply line, a repair is small. If it is a burst section, the burst pipe repair page explains what happens next.
