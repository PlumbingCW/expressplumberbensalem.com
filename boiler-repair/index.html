No heat in winter

A boiler that stops in cold weather puts the whole house at risk. Pipes in exterior walls can freeze within hours once the heat is gone. Call at any hour. Same-day service is offered when the schedule allows.

Safety comes first when the system involves gas or water, so follow these steps. Boiler installation and venting fall under the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the mechanical code, along with the maker's guidelines, so a replacement is a permitted job.

  • !Smell gas or hear a hiss: leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.
  • !Check the thermostat settings and that the boiler switch and breaker are on.
  • !Look at the pressure gauge. Very low or very high readings are a sign of a problem.
  • !Do not open the boiler cabinet or relight a pilot unless you know how.

Repair or replace an older boiler

Many older boilers run for decades with good care. A failed pump, a worn valve or a dirty burner is a repair. A cracked heat exchanger or a boiler that needs a major part every season is a different story, and replacement may be the better value.

Efficiency matters too. Older units burn more fuel than newer high-efficiency models. A plumber can compare a repair with a replacement for your house, with a clear price for each.

Carbon monoxide and boiler safety

Any fuel-burning appliance can produce carbon monoxide if it vents poorly. A working detector near sleeping areas and on each level is inexpensive protection. Test it monthly and replace batteries on schedule.

If a detector sounds, leave the house and call 911. A boiler that soots, smells or has a yellow or orange flame needs service right away. Do not run a boiler you suspect is venting badly.

Pipes at risk when the heat fails

When a boiler stops, the water in the radiators and pipes can freeze in very cold weather. A burst heating pipe is a double problem because the system loses its water and the house loses heat at once. If a pipe splits, see burst pipe repair for the steps.

Keep the house as warm as you can with safe sources, and open cabinet doors on exterior walls. Do not use an oven to heat the house, since it creates a carbon monoxide risk.
