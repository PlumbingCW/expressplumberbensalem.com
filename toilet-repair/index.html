A toilet overflowing right now

An overflowing toilet pushes water and waste across the floor. The first move is to stop the water, not to flush again. Reach behind the bowl and turn the supply valve clockwise, or lift the float in the tank.

Call the emergency line at any hour. If more than one drain is backing up, the main line may be blocked, and that is a different job. Toilet fixtures sold today have to meet water-use standards, and the IPC sets the flange and clearance requirements an installer works to.

  • !Shut the supply valve behind the toilet.
  • !Do not flush again, and stop using other fixtures if water rises elsewhere.
  • !Put towels around the base and keep children and pets away.
  • !Call if water is coming up in the tub or floor drain too, which points to the main line.

The silent leak that raises your bill

A toilet that runs without anyone noticing can waste a large amount of water each day. The sound is faint, and the trickle in the bowl is easy to miss. A few drops of food coloring in the tank show it. If color appears in the bowl without a flush, the flapper is leaking.

Replacing a flapper or fill valve is a short job. Waiting costs money every day. If the bill is already high and the toilet checks out, leak detection looks at the rest of the system.

When to replace instead of repair

Repairs make sense for newer toilets with one failed part. Replacement is the better call when these apply.

  • ✓The porcelain is cracked, even slightly.
  • ✓The toilet needs a repair every few months.
  • ✓Flushing power is weak even after cleaning and adjustments.
  • ✓The toilet is old and wastes water compared with newer models.
  • ✓The floor around the base is soft or stained from past leaks.

Wax rings, floors and what a leak at the base means

A toilet seals to the drain with a wax ring. When it fails, water seeps out around the base during flushing and soaks the subfloor. Many people ignore a damp spot until the floor softens. By then the repair includes the floor.

A rocking toilet is the clue. Reset it with a new ring and check the flange. If the subfloor has already softened, the plumber will tell you what needs to be repaired before the toilet is set again.
