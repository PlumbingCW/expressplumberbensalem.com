Croydon between two waterways

Croydon is a census-designated place in Bristol Township with about 10,000 residents and ZIP code 19021. Neshaminy Creek borders it to the south, and the Delaware River borders it to the east. Many of its homes sit near water, and that shapes the plumbing calls we hear.

If your basement is damp after heavy rain, think about the sump pump first. A pump that has sat idle for months fails in the worst storm. Testing it in the dry season takes a few minutes and tells you whether to repair it or replace it before it is needed. Sump discharge and drain work follow International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards, and the township office can say what a permit needs.

Before we arrive

Take these steps while a plumber is on the way.

Water in the basement

Stay out of standing water near outlets and the electrical panel.

Pump not running

Check the plug and the breaker before you call.

Backed-up drain

Stop all water use until the plumber clears the line.

Burst pipe

Close the main valve and open a low faucet to drain the line.

Local note: a historic building and a creek name

Croydon has one entry on the National Register of Historic Places, White Hall of Bristol College, listed in 1984. It is a reminder that parts of this area are old, and older neighborhoods often have older drain lines.

The Neshaminy Creek that marks its southern edge also forms the eastern boundary of Bensalem Township. During the remnants of Hurricane Ida in September 2021, county officials reported the creek cresting at 20.84 feet. We do not claim that any particular Croydon street flooded in that storm. Ask your neighbors and your township about your block.

Storm season checklist for Croydon homes

Before a forecast of heavy rain, check the sump pump, clear the discharge line and move valuables off the basement floor. Know where the main water shutoff and the electrical panel sit, and keep a flashlight nearby.

If water begins to rise, stay out of the basement when it is near outlets or the panel. Call us, and call your electric utility if water reaches the electrical equipment. A flooded basement is not a place to test whether the power is off.
